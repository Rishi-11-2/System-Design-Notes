Razorpay Notification System Architecture: Multi-Channel Delivery, Dynamic Gateway Routing & OTP Prioritization

In financial payments, a delayed One-Time Password (OTP) or dropped transaction alert directly destroys conversion rates and merchant trust. Discover how Razorpay engineered its notification platform—processing hundreds of millions of daily messages across SMS, WhatsApp, Email, and Webhooks with sub-3-second OTP priority queues, dynamic telecom vendor failover, and zero duplicate alerts.

1. The Scale of Payment Notifications

As India's leading full-stack financial payments infrastructure, Razorpay powers transactions for millions of businesses, from global e-commerce portals to local merchants. In this ecosystem, notifications are not secondary marketing tools—they are mission-critical payment infrastructure:

  • Two-Factor OTP Alerts: During bank checkout flows, an SMS/WhatsApp OTP must arrive within under 3 seconds. If an OTP takes 15 seconds to arrive, the user abandons the checkout window.
  • Merchant Webhooks: Payment success callbacks must reach merchant order management servers reliably to trigger physical product fulfillment and order provisioning.
  • Scale: Hundreds of millions of notifications dispatched daily across SMS, WhatsApp Business API, Email, Webhooks, and Mobile Push.

3. Multi-Tier Priority Queues: Eliminating Head-of-Line Blocking

A common architectural failure in notification engines is a single shared queue. If a merchant triggers a batch marketing campaign sending 500,000 promotional emails, high-priority OTP messages queued behind the batch get delayed by minutes.

Razorpay strictly segregates workloads into Three Priority Tiers:

  • P0 (Critical Real-Time): Authentication OTPs, two-factor challenges, and fraud block alerts. Allocated dedicated, unthrottled Kafka partitions and worker pools. Target latency: < 3 seconds.
  • P1 (Transactional Operational): Payment successful receipts, refund confirmations, and webhook callbacks. Target latency: < 30 seconds.
  • P2 (Asynchronous Batch): Weekly merchant settlements, tax invoices, and promotional announcements. Throttled during peak daytime hours to protect telecom capacity.

4. Dynamic Telecom Vendor Routing & Failover

In emerging markets, telecommunication aggregators (e.g., Gupshup, ValueFirst, Karix, Airtel) suffer frequent degraded network states: localized fiber cuts, telecom switch buffer overflows, and regulatory filtering backlogs.

To maintain 99.99% delivery reliability, Razorpay implements Real-Time Dynamic Routing:

  • Moving-Window Delivery Health: Workers track Delivery Receipts (DLRs) per telecom vendor over a rolling 60-second sliding window.
  • Automated Circuit Breaker: If Vendor A's delivery success rate drops below 92% or average latency exceeds 4,000ms, the routing engine automatically trips a circuit breaker and reroutes 100% of P0 traffic to Vendor B in under 5 seconds.
  • Traffic Shading & Probing: A small canary stream (1% of traffic) continues probing degraded vendors until health metrics recover.

5. Distributed Idempotency & Rate Limiting

Sending duplicate payment alerts or multiple debit SMS messages causes consumer panic and flooded support lines. Razorpay enforces strict idempotency: 

// Distributed Idempotency Guard in Go
func (s *NotificationService) Dispatch(ctx context.Context, req NotificationReq) error {
    idempotencyKey := fmt.Sprintf("notif:%s:%s", req.Channel, req.IdempotencyID)

    // Atomic Redis SETNX with 24-hour lease
    ok, err := s.redis.SetNX(ctx, idempotencyKey, "PROCESSING", 24*time.Hour).Result()
    if err != nil || !ok {
        // Duplicate detected: safe short-circuit return
        return ErrDuplicateNotification
    }

    // Process notification through Kafka priority pipeline
    return s.kafkaProducer.Publish(req)
}

6. Delivery Receipt (DLR) Reconcilers

Telecom operators dispatch asynchronous HTTP webhooks containing Delivery Receipts (DLR) indicating whether a message was delivered, rejected, or expired on the cellular tower.

  • Asynchronous Ingestion: DLR webhooks land on an auto-scaled edge gateway, queuing events into Kafka.
  • Smart Fallback: If an SMS DLR indicates failure due to handset out of coverage or carrier spam filtering, the reconciler automatically initiates a fallback dispatch over WhatsApp or Email.

8. Key Architectural Takeaways

  • Separate Critical OTPs from Batch Traffic: Never share a single queue between time-sensitive authentication tokens and marketing campaigns. Head-of-line blocking destroys checkout conversion rates.
  • Implement Dynamic Multi-Vendor Routing: Telecom providers suffer localized outages regularly. Continuously track real-time DLR latencies and automatically steer traffic to healthy carriers.
  • Distributed Idempotency Prevents Customer Panic: Enforce atomic Redis locks on unique transaction IDs to guarantee consumers are never sent duplicate debit alerts.
  • Leverage Smart Cross-Channel Fallbacks: If cellular SMS channels fail to deliver within 10 seconds, automatically re-route the alert via secondary channels like WhatsApp Business.
